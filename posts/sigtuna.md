Sigtuna is an easy place to like if you enjoy small towns with a long memory. It sits by Lake Mälaren, close enough to Stockholm for a simple day trip, but it feels much quieter and older once you are there.

The town is often described as Sweden's oldest town, founded around the end of the 10th century. That history is visible, but it does not make the place feel heavy. Sigtuna is gentle: lake air, low wooden houses, ruins, runestones, cafes, and small details that reward slow walking.

![Viking boat on the children's playground](sigtuna2.jpg)

The Viking-style boat on the playground was a perfect first sign of the town's personality. Sigtuna knows its history, but it lets it appear in everyday, playful ways too.

## Around the Old Town

The center is small and very walkable. Stora gatan, the old main street, has cafes, shops, and wooden houses that make the town feel warm rather than museum-like.

![Sigtuna - church ruins](sigtuna5.jpg)

The church ruins are where the age of the place becomes more visible. They sit naturally in the town, not sealed away, which makes the history feel close and quiet.

![Sigtuna park](sigtuna4.jpg)

The park gives the walk a softer pause. Sigtuna is good at this: a historical detail, then a green space, then a lake view, then another small street.

## History

Sigtuna was an important royal and trading center in early medieval Sweden. During a period around the turn of the 11th century, Sweden's first coins were minted here, which makes the town feel much more significant than its small size suggests.

The best part is that the history does not arrive as one big monument. It appears in fragments: a sign, a stone, a ruin, an old street name, a view across the water.

![Information board in Sigtuna](sigtuna7.jpg)

The information boards help connect those fragments, but the town still leaves space for wandering. It is not a place that needs to be rushed through point by point.

![A cat lying next to the shop's entrance](sigtuna3.jpg)

And then there was this cat, doing exactly what cats do best: making a shop entrance look like it belongs to them.

![A duck](sigtuna6.jpg)

The duck by the water added to the slow-lake-day mood. Sigtuna is full of these tiny pauses that make the visit feel easy.

![Sigtuna - a runestone in a circle of stones](sigtuna8.jpg)

The runestones are probably the clearest link between the quiet present-day town and its Viking-age past. They make the history feel physical, not abstract.

![Tant Brun cafeteria's signboard](sigtuna9.jpg)

Ending near a cafe sign felt right for Sigtuna. After the runestones and ruins, the town gently returns you to coffee, shops, and the ordinary pleasure of a small Swedish street.
