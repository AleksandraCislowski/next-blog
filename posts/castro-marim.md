Castro Marim is small, but it has the kind of landscape that makes a place feel wide. The town sits near the Guadiana River and the Spanish border, with salt marshes, low hills, and fortress walls giving everything a dry, bright Algarve atmosphere.

It is a good place for a slow historic walk: not crowded, not overwhelming, but full of edges, walls, viewpoints, and little reminders that this was once an important border town.

![Castro Marim landscape](castro2.jpg)

From the first views, Castro Marim feels connected to the land around it. The town does not hide behind its monuments; it opens out toward the marshes and the river.

## Fort São Sebastião

Fort São Sebastião is one of the clearest signs of that defensive past. Built as part of the town's 17th-century military system, it still gives Castro Marim a strong silhouette and a feeling of watching the border.

![Fort São Sebastião](castro5.jpg)

The fort makes the town feel more dramatic than its quiet streets suggest. It is easy to imagine how different this landscape would have felt when distance, visibility, and protection mattered every day.

## Around the Town

![Castro Marim landscape](castro3.jpg)

Outside the main walls, the landscape has a sparse beauty: pale paths, low vegetation, and that strong southern light that makes every line feel sharper.

![Castro Marim landscape building's walls](castro4.jpg)

The old walls are probably what I liked most here. They give the walk a rhythm, guiding you from one view to the next without needing a complicated route.

![Mural in Castro Marim](castro6.jpg)

Then the town softens again with small details like this mural. Castro Marim is not only a fortress stop; it still has the everyday texture of a lived-in place.

![Castro Marim town square](castro7.jpg)

The square brings everything back to a calmer scale. After the fort and the open views, it feels good to end somewhere human-sized, with the sense that the town has more stories than its size would suggest.
