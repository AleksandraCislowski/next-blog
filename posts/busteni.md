Bușteni was part of a one-day route through three Romanian castles: Peleș, Cantacuzino, and Bran. By the time we reached Cantacuzino Castle, the day already had plenty of drama, but this stop felt different from the others. It looked less like a storybook fortress and more like a grand mountain residence, calm and solid on its terrace above the town.

The surprise was how much the setting took over. The castle was beautiful, but the real spectacle was around it: green slopes, dark ridges, low clouds sliding across the Bucegi Mountains, and views that made the whole place feel much bigger than the building itself.

## Cantacuzino Castle

Cantacuzino Castle has a quieter kind of grandeur. It does not try to be sharp and defensive; it sits wide and composed, with stone walls, arcaded windows, terraces, garden paths, and enough symmetry to feel formal without becoming cold.

![Wednesday filming location banner in the garden at Cantacuzino Castle](busteni2.jpg)

There was also the funny modern layer: Cantacuzino Castle is known from Netflix's Wednesday, where it was used for exterior shots of Nevermore Academy. The banner in the garden made that connection impossible to miss, but the place still had its own atmosphere beyond the screen reference.

![Cantacuzino Castle above a stone terrace wall under a bright blue sky](busteni3.jpg)

From below, the castle looked more like a residence than a fortress. The heavy stone terrace gave it weight, while the tower, arcades, and surrounding trees made it feel elegant rather than severe.

![Cantacuzino Castle seen across lawns, stone steps, and garden paths](busteni4.jpg)

The approach through the grounds made the building feel especially settled into the hillside. Stone steps, clipped shrubs, grass, and forest behind it all softened the architecture.

![Side view of Cantacuzino Castle with stone walls, red roofs, and a covered terrace](busteni5.jpg)

This side view showed why the castle felt so residential to me. It had the scale of a major estate, but the red roofs, garden paths, and wide terrace made it feel made for looking outward, not locking itself away.

![Tall stone tower and main entrance of Cantacuzino Castle](busteni6.jpg)

The tower brought back a little more castle energy. Even so, the details were controlled and elegant: pale stone trim, narrow windows, carved panels, wooden doors, and a clean vertical shape against the sky.

Inside the courtyard, the building felt warmer. The red roof tiles, arched windows, stone walls, and flowered decoration made the space feel more intimate after the grander exterior views.

![Castle courtyard with arched windows, clipped shrubs, and a souvenir shop sign](busteni8.jpg)

The courtyard had that practical tourist rhythm too: open doors, a souvenir sign, people moving through, and neat shrubs around the cobblestones. It kept the castle from feeling distant or overly precious.

## Mountain Terrace

The terrace was the real reason this stop stayed with me. The castle was interesting, especially with the Wednesday connection, but the view was the moment that made Bușteni feel unforgettable.

![White fountain and terrace tables facing cloudy mountains above Bușteni](busteni9.jpg)

From the terrace, everything opened at once. The white fountain and cafe tables were pretty, but the mountains behind them completely stole the scene, with clouds catching on the peaks like they had been placed there for drama.

![Round fountain and green garden below steep mountains with low clouds](busteni10.jpg)

The garden looked almost unreal from above: pale water in the fountain, bright spring grass, dark forest, and then the steep mountain wall rising behind the town. It made the castle feel like a viewing platform as much as a destination.

![Jagged mountain peaks and low clouds above a row of tall evergreen trees](busteni11.jpg)

The clouds made the mountains feel alive. They moved around the ridges and hid parts of the peaks, so the view kept changing between sharp rock, snow patches, green forest, and white mist.

![Wide view of Bușteni below the Bucegi Mountains with clouds over the peaks](busteni12.jpg)

This was the view I would remember most from the whole stop. Bușteni sat below in small rooftops and trees, while the mountains behind it looked enormous, close, and completely overwhelming in the best way.

![Clouds wrapping around dark mountain peaks above green forest](busteni13.jpg)

The longer I looked, the more dramatic the mountains became. The peaks were partly hidden, but that made them better, because the clouds gave the whole landscape a wild, moving quality.

![Forest hillside with railway tracks below a bright spring sky](busteni14.jpg)

Even the lower slopes were beautiful. The hillside was a mix of fresh green leaves and darker conifers, with the railway line cutting through the bottom like a reminder that this was still a real mountain town, not just a viewpoint.

## Around Bușteni

After the castle, Bușteni itself kept the mountain mood going. The roads, villas, roofs, and trees all sat under the same dramatic backdrop, so even ordinary street views felt scenic.

![Historic villas with green roofs below misty mountain slopes](busteni15.jpg)

The buildings around town had their own mountain-resort character: steep roofs, green metal, timber, stone, hedges, and misty slopes behind them. It made the place feel like it belonged to the landscape.

![Half-timbered building with a rounded tower and green roof under a cloudy sky](busteni16.jpg)

This building looked especially charming, with dark timber lines, pale walls, a rounded tower, and a weathered green roof. It had a very different style from Cantacuzino Castle, but still felt tied to the same mountain setting.

![Quiet road in Bușteni with villas, trees, and mountains ahead](busteni17.jpg)

The road views were lovely in a calmer way. Villas on both sides, wet pavement, hedges, and mountains straight ahead made the town feel like it was constantly pointing you back toward the peaks.

![Mountain road view with green-roofed buildings and low clouds over the slopes](busteni18.jpg)

This final view summed Bușteni up well: elegant old buildings, green roofs, a quiet road, and clouds still hanging over the mountains. For a one-day trip with Peleș and Bran on the same route, Bușteni could easily have become just the middle stop. Instead, it gave the day its biggest views. Cantacuzino Castle may have felt more like a residence than a classic castle, but with those mountains around it, that was exactly what made it special.
